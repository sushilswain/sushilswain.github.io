Search Query Intelligence System

A governed workflow for Google Ads search terms: collect visible queries, prepare recommendations, require human approval, check the live account again and record the outcome.

  • Role: System architect and implementation owner
  • Google Ads Scripts, Sheets and AI-assisted review
  • August–September 2026

01 · Problem

A bad negative can hide good demand

A search term can be irrelevant to one campaign but valuable to another integration use case. Applying negatives from a loose classification risks blocking the right buyer. As the account grew, every proposed change needed context, an approval decision and a traceable record.

Google Ads discloses only part of Search activity. One August account snapshot showed disclosed terms covering 12 of 61 clicks. The workflow therefore acts only on what it can see and check.

02 · Design

A recommendation never applies itself

  1. Collect disclosed search terms
  2. Prepare a recommendation
  3. Human approves a specific action
  4. Recheck, apply and log

A scheduled Google Ads Script writes queries and account context to a shared ledger. AI-assisted review proposes KEEP, WATCH, NEGATIVE or ROUTE. It has no direct Google Ads credential path. A human accepts the proposed row; one executor pass binds the approved recommendation without changing Ads. A later pass may apply it only after fresh checks.

If approval, target or account context is stale or invalid, the executor records the stop and makes no Ads change.

03 · Safeguards

Controls at the point of action

Specific approval

The reviewed action is fingerprinted. A changed or stale approval cannot silently become a different account action.

Fresh account checks

The executor rechecks target status, conversion protection, negative overlap and routing destination immediately before mutation.

Limited scope

Exact-match negatives are the constrained default. Automated broad, shared-list and account-level negative execution are disabled.

Recorded outcomes

The ledger and change log preserve applied, skipped and no-action outcomes so a decision can be reviewed later.

04 · Testing

Proving the workflow before scaling it

Production acceptance used a dry run, one controlled live exact negative, independent recollection of that negative and an idempotency rerun that did not repeat the action. The later audit hardened approval, routing, recovery and collector behavior.

Regression checks passed 42/42 for the executor and 9/9 for collector hardening. A real September data-type mismatch invalidated approval fingerprints; the scheduled executor failed closed and made no Ads change in that event. The reviewed rows were repaired and human approvals rebound before execution resumed.

05 · Result

A working decision and execution system

On 27 September, the live control sheet reported successful collector and executor runs, data collected through 26 September and 9 applied actions in the system ledger. The result is an operating workflow with human authority, fresh checks and a recorded action trail.

The next outcome question is whether these decisions improve qualified demand or reduce review time. Those downstream effects have not yet been established with a measured comparison.

06 · Learning

The important design decision was authority

Classification helps prepare a decision. A dependable system also needs to know who approved it, whether the context changed and what actually happened in Ads. Treating a failed check as an explicit outcome made the workflow easier to trust and repair.
